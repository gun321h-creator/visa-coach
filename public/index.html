How it works

  1. 01

    File the form

    A short DS-160: purpose, occupation, who pays, relatives in the U.S., prior travel, return plan, ties. Eight fields, about a minute. You can skip it — the interview still runs, it just has nothing to check you against.

  2. 02

    He cross-examines it

    Officer Parker is not learning about you. Every question he asks already has an answer on his screen, and he is checking whether your mouth agrees with your paperwork. Diverge, and he interrupts and quotes both sides back at you.

  3. 03

    See every gap

    The report leads with what you filed beside what you said, and why an officer would refuse over the difference. Then the scorecard, the lines that cost you, and the fixes.

What is on his screen

Every field you file is a question he can already check the answer to. This list is generated from the form itself.

    What you get at the end

    Not a grade. A list of the things a real officer could refuse you over — starting with the ones your own form contradicts.

    2 likely refusal reasons found

    “Likely refusal. You contradicted your own form twice, and the second time you could not explain it.”
